About this assignment

This assignment follows the two-phase PM workflow from the course: Discovery — understand the problem — and Definition — shape the solution. Each section is self-contained. Work through them in order.

The topic is a new product concept: an identity and trust system for AI agents. Today, brands, users, and machines are identified by phone numbers, email addresses, domains, and IP addresses. Tomorrow, AI agents acting on behalf of brands and individuals will need their own identity layer — and no such standard exists yet. Your job is to define what that product looks like.

The Agentic Shift

AI agents are now appearing across every channel — embedded in websites, operating inside WhatsApp, answering phone calls, and running autonomously in APIs. Both brands and individuals are deploying agents. But none of them have a standard identity. There is no equivalent of a phone number or domain for an agent.

The gap: when an agent contacts you on WhatsApp, calls you, or acts on your behalf — there is currently no way to verify who it is, who controls it, whether it is legitimate, or whether it has been compromised. Identity solves this.

The Stakeholders

Agent identity affects every participant in a communication — not just the deploying brand. Each stakeholder has a different relationship to the identity problem.

2.3
Product Definition

Define the product at a strategic level across four components. This is about the what and why — not the feature detail.

Section schema — four components
Product Category
What type of product is this — a registry, a certification authority, a trust network, a platform, an open standard? This choice determines the business model, the governance structure, and who has authority over identity decisions.
Primary Customer & Scenario
Who is the first customer, and what problem do they solve on day one? Describe one concrete scenario — what the world looks like before and after they use this product.
Identity Model
What IS an agent identity? What fields define it — what does a record contain? What makes an agent ID unique? How do you handle the same agent operating across multiple channels?
Trust & Security Model
How is legitimacy established — static verification or dynamic behaviour scoring? What distinguishes a legitimate agent from a spam agent, a bot, or an impersonator? What triggers investigation or revocation?
The Identity Model and Trust Model are the hardest and most important components. A registry without a clear trust model is just a list — it creates the appearance of verification without the substance.
~1 page

2.5
Roadmap

Build a three-horizon roadmap for the Agent Identity Platform. Identity platforms have strong network effects — the registry is only valuable when enough agents are registered. Your roadmap must reflect how you build supply (registered agents) and demand (channels that check identity) in parallel.

Section schema
Now
0–3 months
What can a brand or developer do with this product on day one?
→
Next
3–6 months
What becomes possible once there's a critical mass of registered agents?
→
Later
6–12 months
What does the full platform enable once trust signals are accumulating over time?
Not building (1 item) One capability deliberately excluded from this 12-month window + 2-sentence justification
The chicken-and-egg problem is real: channels won't check agent identity if few agents are registered; brands won't register if no channels check. Your "Now" horizon must address which side you seed first and why.
